Research

Climate, water, and the shaping of planetary surfaces

My thesis research asks one question in three settings: what can a landscape tell us about the climate that shaped it? I combine remote sensing data analysis, numerical modelling, fieldwork, and theory to connect climate model predictions to what we actually observe on planetary surfaces.

  • Mars

    Martian valley networks and paleolake basins record a period when liquid water flowed across the surface. I constrain the discharge and precipitation regimes required to incise valleys and form paleolakes, to answer questions about early Martian hydrology and climate.

  • Titan

    Titan’s methane-based hydrological cycle is an extreme analogue to Earth’s water cycle. Using an oceanic general circulation model, I simulate how seasonal precipitation and evaporation drive circulation in Titan’s northern polar seas.

  • Earth

    Earth is the only planet where we can ground-truth landscape evolution theory. I use high-resolution remote sensing and landscape evolution modelling to calibrate the models we apply elsewhere in the Solar System.
