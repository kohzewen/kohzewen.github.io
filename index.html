Hello! My name is Ze-Wen, and I’m a Ph.D student in Planetary Science at MIT. I work with Prof. Gaia Stucky de Quay in the Gaia Lab, where I study the formation and evolution of planetary surfaces.

Currently, my work focuses on the interactions between climatic forcing and landscape evolution on Earth, Mars, and Titan. By leveraging remote sensing data analysis, numerical modelling, fieldwork, and theory, I aim to bridge the gap between climate predictions and planetary surface observations.

Outside of research, I am passionate about teaching and outreach, particularly in improving the accessibility of STEM and academia for underrepresented communities. Please feel free to reach out via email if you have any questions or inquiries!

Research themes

Mars

Valley networks & ancient climate

Constraining the hydrology that carved Martian valleys, and what it implies for early climate.

 Titan

Methane seas & circulation

Ocean-model simulations of seasonal circulation in Titan’s northern polar seas.

 Earth

Terrestrial analogues

Fieldwork and remote sensing to calibrate landscape-evolution theory on a planet we can visit.
